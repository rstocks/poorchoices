I will say this in my own defence: it was on sale.

It was on sale, and a man at the shop said &ldquo;you'll find a way,&rdquo; and I, an adult
with a credit card and a documented history of overestimating my own resourcefulness, agreed
that I would find a way.

## The stairwell

The kayak is **3.6 metres long**. The stairwell, measured later with a tape measure I had
to buy specifically for the purpose of this measurement, accommodates objects up to roughly
**3.6 metres long**, provided the object can be persuaded to bend slightly around the
landing on the second floor.

A kayak cannot be persuaded to bend. I want to be very clear about that.

## Susan

Susan lives in 2B. Susan opened her door because, and I quote, &ldquo;it sounded like
something was *dying* out here.&rdquo; Susan now waves to me in the lobby in a way I would
describe as *cautious*. We are not, I think, friends. But we are neighbours, which is
arguably more durable.

## The kayak today

The kayak lives in my living room. It is, in fairness, an excellent piece of furniture.
Guests sit in it. I have written most of this post from it. I have not yet taken it to
any body of water. That is a problem for the version of me who figures out the stairwell
problem in reverse.
